# Hormuz: controlled passage

Version 1 · September 2026

## Positioning

Hormuz is an open-source AI policy gateway for supported requests routed through it, with budgets, secret controls, and content-free routine evidence.

Primary audience: platform and engineering teams adopting AI coding clients under company provider accounts. Security reviewers and operators need inspectable boundaries and clear responsibility.

Lead message: **Give your team AI. Keep control.**

Brand concept: **Controlled passage.** Teams need room to work with AI and organizations need a clear place to apply their rules. An open channel through deliberate boundaries expresses those needs together. This is a design metaphor, not a claim about the origin of the name or a literal architecture diagram.

## Identity

The established three-bar circular mark remains the core identity. The wordmark uses outlined Geist Mono lettering. Supplied SVGs require no fonts to display.

- `hormuz-lockup.svg`: forest, for light backgrounds.
- `hormuz-lockup-reverse.svg`: chalk, for dark backgrounds.
- `hormuz-mark.svg`: standalone mark.
- `icon.svg`: dedicated small browser icon.
- `social-preview.png`: 1200 × 630 link/organic-post artwork.
- `controlled-passage.webp`: original abstract editorial artwork, 1536 × 1024.
- `tokens.css`: reusable palette and font roles.

Keep clear space of at least half the mark's diameter around the lockup. Minimum standalone mark size: 24 px. Use the dedicated favicon below that. Recommended minimum lockup width: 150 px. Keep proportions, contrast, and supplied colors. Use a solid quiet background. Avoid stretching, shadows, busy image overlays, or converting the mark into a status indicator.

Use ink or forest text on chalk, sage, or signal. Use chalk or signal on forest. Signal is an accent, not body text on a light background. Include words or symbols alongside status colors. Check contrast for each actual pairing and size.

## Typography and layout

Geist for headings, body, and interfaces. Geist Mono for labels, code, and inspectable details. Georgia italic for a short editorial phrase; never code, controls, or dense body copy. Website fonts load through Next's existing font pipeline. Documents use Helvetica/Arial-compatible sans and a serif accent for portable Office/PDF rendering.

Use generous outer space, a consistent grid, fine borders, and rounded surfaces. Keep short headlines large. Keep evidence and legal boundaries readable rather than treating them as decoration. Motion should explain state or focus, respect reduced-motion preferences, and never simulate live activity.

## Visual language

Parallel route lines and clear checkpoints can connect an editorial section, a resource cover, or an end-of-page invitation. Keep them separate from functional architecture diagrams, which must label the actual data flow. The original layered artwork provides a tactile expression of the same channel idea. Crop it without distortion. Avoid literal maps, shipping imagery, robots, locks, and generic circuit-board graphics.

## Voice

Be specific, calm, and helpful. Start with the user's workflow. Describe what changes, what evidence can be inspected, and the next decision.

- “Keep Codex and Claude Code. Put policy in the request path.”
- “Company provider keys stay on the gateway.”
- “Bring one workflow. We'll work through it together.”

Never turn an illustrative diagram into a customer claim. Preserve the distinction between the self-hosted open core, scoped pilot services, and separately agreed support. Do not imply universal AI coverage, certification, managed hosting, a guaranteed outcome, or a 24/7 service. Product evidence and terms remain the source of truth.

## Applications

The website uses the same mark in navigation, product illustrations, and resource covers. Interior pages share the route motif while retaining task-specific layouts. Briefs and the buyer deck carry the same color and identity. The social image supports organic sharing; a paid placement still needs copy and destination appropriate to its campaign.
